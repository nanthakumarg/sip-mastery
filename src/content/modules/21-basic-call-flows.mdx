## 21.1 Registration, with and without a challenge

Part 8 is a library of call flows. Each one is a full interactive ladder: play it, step through it, and click a message to open it in the inspector, which also marks the headers that changed since the previous message. The flows follow RFC 3665, the IETF's set of basic call flow examples. RFC 3665 itself warns that its messages are simplified:

<RfcQuote id="rfc3665-1.3-not-copied" />

The messages in this course are complete. The course's protocol checker reads every one of them, with the same rules as in the earlier modules, so you can copy them.

A call starts long before the INVITE: Bob's phone must register, or Proxy B cannot find it. The REGISTER goes to the <Term t="Registrar" /> of Bob's domain, which stores the <Term t="binding" /> and answers 200 OK with every binding of the AOR (Module 12). On a trusted network, for example a phone that the registrar knows by its IP address or its TLS certificate, that is all. On the public Internet, the registrar first challenges with 401, and the phone sends the REGISTER again with credentials (Module 13). The left ladder also shows a refresh, a query, and a removal (RFC 3665 §2.2 to §2.4):

<CompareFlows a="register-lifecycle" b="register-401" labels={['Without a challenge', 'With a 401 challenge']} />

In every registration trace, check three things:

- The Request-URI is the domain, with no user part. To and From hold the AOR.
- The retry after the 401 has CSeq one higher, the same Call-ID, and a new branch.
- The 200 OK lists all the bindings, each with its own expiry. The phone refreshes before the expiry in the 200 OK, not before the one it asked for.

## 21.2 Direct call between two user agents

The simplest call has no server at all: Alice's phone sends the INVITE straight to Bob's phone, as in RFC 3665 §3.1. Real networks rarely do this, because Alice's phone would need Bob's current address. But every other flow in this module builds on it:

1. **INVITE**: Alice's phone opens the INVITE transaction. The SDP offer lists the codecs it can use.
2. **180 Ringing**: Bob's phone rings. The To tag in the 180 creates an <Term t="early dialog" />.
3. **200 OK**: Bob answers. The SDP answer chooses PCMU, and the dialog is now confirmed.
4. **ACK**: Alice's phone confirms the 200 OK. This ACK is a new transaction, with a new branch.
5. **RTP**: the audio flows between the two phones, to the addresses in the SDP.
6. **BYE** and **200 OK**: either side hangs up, and the dialog ends.

This is the **call flow builder**. Every option above the ladder changes the call, and the ladder and every message change with it. Try *Lost packet: The 200 OK*: Bob's phone sends the 200 OK again and again until an ACK arrives, because only the UAS core can make a 2xx reliable:

## 21.3 Call through one proxy, then through two proxies with Record-Route

With **one proxy**, Alice's phone sends the INVITE to Proxy B, the proxy for biloxi.example; DNS gives its address (Module 11). Proxy B answers 100 Trying, finds Bob's Contact in its <Term t="location service" />, puts that Contact in the Request-URI, adds its own Via, and forwards the INVITE. The responses come back along the Via headers. Without Record-Route, Proxy B is out of the dialog after the 200 OK: the ACK and the BYE go straight to Bob's Contact.

<FlowBuilder kind="call" preset={{ path: 'proxy', recordRoute: false }} eyebrow="Flow builder · one proxy, no Record-Route" />

With **two proxies** (RFC 3665 §3.2), Alice's phone sends everything to Proxy A, its <Term t="outbound proxy" />, with a <Term t="pre-loaded Route">pre-loaded Route</Term> header. Proxy A removes its Route entry and forwards the INVITE to Proxy B. Both proxies add Record-Route, so both stay in the path of every in-dialog request:

<RfcQuote id="rfc3665-3.2-record-route" />

<FlowBuilder kind="call" preset={{ path: 'two-proxies' }} eyebrow="Flow builder · RFC 3665 §3.2: two proxies with Record-Route" />

Switch Record-Route off and on, and watch the ACK and the BYE: with it, they follow the <Term t="route set" />; without it, they go straight to Bob's Contact. The INVITE and its responses always pass through both proxies.

Some messages go one hop at a time. Others go from one end of the call to the other:

| Message | Sent by | How far it goes |
|---|---|---|
| 100 Trying | Each proxy | One hop: back to the element that sent the INVITE |
| 180, 183, 2xx to the INVITE | Bob's phone | Back to Alice's phone, along the Via headers |
| 3xx to 6xx to the INVITE | The element that ends the call | Back to Alice's phone, with an **ACK on each hop** |
| ACK for a 2xx | Alice's phone | End to end: along the route set, or straight to the Contact |
| CANCEL, and its 200 OK | Alice's phone, then each proxy | One hop each |
| BYE, and its 200 OK | Either phone | End to end, like the ACK for a 2xx |

## 21.4 Call with a 407 challenge

Proxy A accepts calls only from its own users, so it challenges the first INVITE with 407 Proxy Authentication Required. This is RFC 3665 §3.2 in full: Module 13 explained the digest; here it is in the context of the whole call.

<RfcQuote id="rfc3261-22.3-challenge-407" />

<FlowBuilder kind="call" preset={{ path: 'two-proxies', auth: true }} eyebrow="Flow builder · RFC 3665 §3.2: a 407 challenge" />

Follow the CSeq: 1 for the first INVITE and the ACK for the 407, 2 for the INVITE with credentials and the ACK for the 200 OK, and 3 for Alice's BYE. The Call-ID and the From tag never change. Proxy A removes the Proxy-Authorization header before it forwards the INVITE, because those credentials are for its own realm only. Proxy B never sees them.

## 21.5 Busy (486), no answer (480/408), rejected (603), not found (404)

A call can fail at Bob's phone or at a proxy. In each case the failure response travels back to Alice's phone, and each hop confirms it with its own ACK:

<RfcQuote id="rfc3665-3.9-hop-by-hop" />

| Outcome | Code | Sent by | When | RFC 3665 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Busy | 486 Busy Here | Bob's phone | At once: Bob is on another call | §3.9 |
| No answer | 480 Temporarily Unavailable | Bob's phone | After ringing: the phone's no-answer timer, or "do not disturb" | §3.11 |
| No response | 408 Request Timeout | Proxy B | 32 s (Timer B) after an INVITE that nothing answers | §3.10 (shown with 480) |
| Declined | 603 Decline | Bob's phone | Bob presses Reject | — |
| Not found | 404 Not Found | Proxy B | The user does not exist in the domain | — |

Pick an outcome in the builder:

<FlowBuilder kind="call" preset={{ path: 'two-proxies', outcome: 'busy' }} eyebrow="Flow builder · failure responses" />

<RfcQuote id="rfc3261-21.4.18-480" />

"No answer" has three possible ends, depending on which timer fires first:

- **Bob's phone** stops ringing after its own no-answer timer and sends 480 (RFC 3665 §3.11).
- **A proxy** has a ring timeout of its own. When it fires, the proxy cancels the branch to Bob and answers Alice itself, often with 408 or 480. RFC 3261 calls the proxy's INVITE timer <Term t="Timer C" />:

  <RfcQuote id="rfc3261-16.8-timer-c" />

- **Alice** gives up first and hangs up: that is a CANCEL (21.6). RFC 3665 §3.8 calls this flow "Unsuccessful No Answer".

"No response" is different: nothing answers at all, not even a 180. Bob's phone is unplugged, but its binding has not expired yet. The proxy retransmits the INVITE until Timer B fires. RFC 3665 shows a 480 here:

<RfcQuote id="rfc3665-3.10-no-response" />

A proxy can send either code. A 408 says that the request timed out; a 480 says that Bob is unavailable. Many proxies let you choose, and some phones show the two differently.

A **603** means that Bob does not want the call, on any device. A forking proxy that receives a 6xx cancels its other branches and tries no new ones (21.10). A **404** means that the user does not exist; for a user who exists but has no binding, a proxy sends 480 (see Common mistakes).

## 21.6 The caller cancels: CANCEL → 200 (CANCEL) → 487 → ACK

Alice hangs up while Bob's phone rings. Her phone sends a CANCEL, which Proxy A answers itself and then sends on, hop by hop, with the branch of its own INVITE on that hop. The 200 OK to the CANCEL means only "CANCEL received". The INVITE ends with **487 Request Terminated**, which travels back like any other failure response, with an ACK on each hop:

<FlowBuilder kind="call" preset={{ path: 'two-proxies', outcome: 'cancel' }} eyebrow="Flow builder · RFC 3665 §3.8: Alice cancels" />

Two rules from Module 5 and Module 8 matter here. A phone sends a CANCEL only after it has received a provisional response; before that, the CANCEL may arrive before the INVITE. And a CANCEL has no effect once the INVITE has a final response. If a 200 OK crosses the CANCEL, the call is up, and Alice's phone must end it properly:

<RfcQuote id="rfc3665-3.8-crossed" />

## 21.7 Early media (183 with SDP)

<Term t="early media" /> is media before the call is answered: a ringback tone from the far network, an announcement such as "the number you called is not available", or an IVR. Bob's side sends **183 Session Progress** with the SDP answer, and starts RTP at once. The 183 creates an early dialog, like a 180:

<RfcQuote id="rfc3261-13.3.1.1-progress" />

<FlowBuilder kind="call" preset={{ path: 'two-proxies', earlyMedia: true }} eyebrow="Flow builder · early media in a 183" />

Alice's phone must decide what to play: its own <Term t="ringback tone" />, or the media that arrives. RFC 3960 gives the rule:

<RfcQuote id="rfc3960-3.2-play-early-media" />

The SIP messages alone do not tell the phone whether media will come, so a good phone also checks for incoming RTP:

<RfcQuote id="rfc3960-3.2-check-media" />

Without 100rel (Module 22), a 183 is not reliable: it can be lost, and nobody resends it. That is why the 200 OK repeats the same SDP, with the same version in the o= line. Try *Outcome: Declines (603)* with early media on: carriers use exactly this to play an announcement before a failure response.

## 21.8 Late offer (SDP in the 200 OK and in the ACK)

In a <Term t="late offer" />, the INVITE has no SDP. Bob's 200 OK carries the offer, and Alice's ACK carries the answer. RFC 3665 §3.6 uses it, and so do some PBXs, gateways, and third-party call control (Module 22).

<RfcQuote id="rfc3261-13.2.1-patterns" />

<FlowBuilder kind="call" preset={{ path: 'two-proxies', lateOffer: true }} eyebrow="Flow builder · late offer" />

Three consequences:

- The ACK must carry the answer. An ACK without SDP leaves Bob with no address and no codec for Alice.
- Alice cannot reject the offer in the ACK. If nothing in it suits her, she must answer anyway, and then end the call with BYE or change it with a re-INVITE.
- Bob cannot send early media: he has no offer to answer before the 200 OK. (A 183 with an offer needs 100rel and PRACK, Module 22.)

## 21.9 Redirect (302)

A <Term t="Redirect server" /> does not forward the INVITE. It looks up the target and returns it in the Contact header of a 3xx response. The caller then tries the new address itself:

<RfcQuote id="rfc3261-21.3.3-302" />

<FlowBuilder kind="call" preset={{ path: 'redirect' }} eyebrow="Flow builder · a redirect, then a new INVITE" />

The new INVITE keeps the Call-ID, the From tag, and the To header (still without a tag), and uses CSeq 2 and a new branch: it is a new transaction in the same call attempt (RFC 3261 §8.1.3.4). The Redirect server keeps no state for the call, and it is never in the dialog. RFC 3665 §3.6 combines a redirect with a proxy and a late offer.

## 21.10 Forking: parallel and sequential

When Proxy B finds more than one Contact for Bob's AOR, it can try them all at once, or one after another:

<RfcQuote id="rfc3261-16.6-parallel-sequential" />

**Parallel forking** rings every device. Each one that rings makes its own early dialog with Alice's phone, with its own To tag. The first 2xx wins; Proxy B forwards it and cancels every other branch that is still ringing:

<RfcQuote id="rfc3261-16.7-cancel-branches" />

<FlowBuilder kind="call" preset={{ path: 'fork-parallel' }} eyebrow="Flow builder · parallel forking" />

**Sequential forking** ("hunting") tries one device at a time, usually in the order of the q-values in the Contact headers. Proxy B gives each device a ring time, then cancels it and tries the next. The 487 from a cancelled device stays at Proxy B: Alice's phone sees only the early dialogs, one after the other. Switch the path to *Forking: sequential* to compare.

A 6xx stops a forked call completely:

<RfcQuote id="rfc3261-16.7-6xx" />

Two devices can still both answer, if their 200 OKs cross the CANCEL. Module 9 shows how Alice's phone handles that: it ACKs both, and ends the extra dialog with a BYE.

## Common mistakes

<Mistakes>
  <Mistake title="No ACK after the 487 that follows a CANCEL">
    A softphone sends a CANCEL, receives the 200 OK for it, and treats the call as over. When the 487 arrives, it sends nothing. Proxy B retransmits the 487 for 32 seconds; some proxies log each one as an error, and an SBC may count the call as still open. The phone sees no problem at all.

    <BrokenFixedFlow broken="cancel-487-no-ack" fixed="cancel-before-answer" />

    <p class="fix"><b>Remember:</b> the 200 OK ends the CANCEL transaction, not the INVITE transaction. The INVITE gets its own final response, the 487, and every final response to an INVITE needs an ACK.</p>
  </Mistake>

  <Mistake title="Local ringback while the network also sends early media">
    A phone plays its own ringback tone whenever it gets a 180 or a 183, and starts to play RTP only after the 200 OK. Calls to the phone network sound normal, until a number is out of service: the network plays an announcement in early media, the caller hears ringing, and the call fails with no explanation. The opposite mistake is a phone that mutes its ringback after a 183 and then gets no RTP at all: the caller hears silence and hangs up.

    <CompareFlows a="early-media-local-ringback" b="early-media-183" labels={['Problem: local ringback', 'Fixed: play the early media']} />

    <p class="fix"><b>Remember:</b> after a 180 with no incoming media, play local ringback. When RTP arrives in an early dialog, play it and stop the local tone (RFC 3960 §3.2). Do not decide from the response code alone.</p>
  </Mistake>

  <Mistake title="Late offer: an ACK without the answer">
    A gateway sends INVITEs without SDP. A new PBX answers with an offer in the 200 OK, as it should. The gateway sends a plain ACK, because its ACK code was written for early offers only. The call connects, and neither side hears anything: the PBX has no address to send media to.

    <BrokenFixedFlow broken="late-offer-ack-empty" fixed="sdp-late-offer" />

    <p class="fix"><b>Remember:</b> if the INVITE had no SDP, the 2xx has the offer, and the ACK must have the answer. The course's checker calls this rule late-offer-ack.</p>
  </Mistake>

  <Mistake title="404 for a user who exists but is offline">
    A proxy answers 404 whenever the location service has no binding. Bob's phone reboots, and for a minute every caller hears "number does not exist". Some carriers and PBXs then mark the number as invalid, or stop forwarding to voicemail, because 404 says that the user does not exist at all.

    <RfcQuote id="rfc3261-16.5-empty-480" />

    <p class="fix"><b>Remember:</b> 404 for a user that the domain does not know; 480 for a known user with no binding. On the public Internet, consider answering both the same way, so that an attacker cannot use the difference to list your users (Module 14).</p>
  </Mistake>
</Mistakes>

## Summary

- **Registration**: REGISTER to the registrar, 200 OK with every binding. On the Internet, a 401 challenge first, and a retry with credentials, CSeq + 1, and a new branch.
- **A basic call**: INVITE with the offer, 180 (early dialog), 200 OK with the answer (confirmed dialog), ACK, media, BYE.
- **Proxies** answer 100 Trying, add Via, and forward. The last proxy puts Bob's Contact in the Request-URI. With **Record-Route**, they stay in the path of the ACK and the BYE; without it, those go straight to the Contact.
- **Hop by hop**: 100 Trying, CANCEL, and the ACK for a failure response. **End to end**: the 2xx, its ACK, and BYE.
- **Failures**: 486 busy, 480 unavailable or no answer, 408 no response, 603 declined, 404 not found. Each one is ACKed on every hop.
- **CANCEL** gets a 200 OK at each hop, then the INVITE ends with **487**, which still needs an ACK. If a 200 OK crosses the CANCEL, send ACK and then BYE.
- **Early media**: a 183 with the SDP answer, and RTP before the answer. Play it instead of local ringback.
- **Late offer**: the offer in the 200 OK, the answer in the ACK.
- **Redirect**: a 302 with a new Contact, an ACK, then a new INVITE with CSeq + 1 straight to that Contact.
- **Forking**: parallel rings all and cancels the rest after the first 2xx; sequential tries one at a time. Each device makes its own early dialog.
